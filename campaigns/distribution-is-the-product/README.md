# DISTRIBUTION IS THE PRODUCT
### A campaign for Default — hero film: **"THE CURRENT"**

> *Anyone can build the bulb. Own the current.*

---

## 1. The idea in one breath

Everyone remembers Edison for the light bulb. But the bulb was the easy part.
What made Edison *Edison* was the **system** around it: the dynamos, the copper
under Manhattan, the meter on the wall, the reels, the film stock, the theaters.
He won by owning the road between the invention and the customer.

The one time he lost (the War of the Currents), he lost to a current that could
**travel farther**. Even his defeat proves the point: **the product that reaches
the customer wins.**

In 2026 the AI race is running the same play at a thousand times the speed.
Models are commodities, features get copied in a week, and every startup is
building the same bulb. The race isn't to invent. It's to **reach the buyer
first**, in the seconds after they raise their hand.

Default is the current: **Inbound → Enrichment → Qualification → Routing →
Scheduling**, run as one system by your team and its agents.

## 2. Two races, one clock

The film cuts between two eras. Each has its own look and sound, and a single
ticking clock connects them.

| | **THE INDUSTRIAL RACE** (1879–1915) | **THE INTELLIGENCE RACE** (2026) |
|---|---|---|
| Look | Black & white, nitrate grain, 18fps flicker, hard tungsten | Color: warm Kodachrome amber with cold UI light |
| Sound | Pocket-watch escapement, dynamo hum, projector clatter, telegraph | Form-submit click, calendar chime, server hum, cursor tick |
| The bulb | Incandescent lamp, camera, projector | AI product, model, feature |
| The current | Copper conduit, film reels, General Film trucks, AC lines | Form → enrichment → routing → meeting |
| Unit of time | Years | Seconds |
| The moat | Patents + physical infrastructure | Speed-to-buyer + owned GTM system |
| The gatekeeper | A cartel (the Trust) | Nobody. Anyone can own their current now. |
| Who wins | Whoever owns the wire | Whoever reaches the customer first |

**The clock is the spine.** It starts as Edison's pocket watch, becomes the
shutter of a projector, then a switchboard relay, then the tick of a form
submission. It speeds up the whole film (a Shepard-tone build, Nolan style) and
**stops dead** on the logo. The silence is the final beat.

## 3. Getting the history right

The ad's credibility depends on the history being accurate. These facts are
checked, and three of them change the brief:

- **Pearl Street Station opened on 4 Sept 1882** in Lower Manhattan with 82
  customers and about 400 lamps. Edison built the whole system: dynamos,
  underground copper conduit, wiring, switches, fixtures, and a chemical meter
  so he could bill. *This is the "system, not the bulb" proof.*
- **The MPPC ("Edison Trust")** formed in Dec 1908 and pooled the patents. Kodak
  agreed to sell raw film stock only to members. **General Film Company**
  (1910) monopolized US distribution. Exhibitors paid license fees to the
  Trust.
- ⚠️ **Correction: AC vs DC was not Edison's moat. It was his loss.** Edison
  backed DC. Westinghouse and Tesla's AC won because it could be **transmitted
  over long distances** (Niagara Falls, 1896). This makes the story *stronger*:
  AC won on **distribution**, not invention. The script uses it as the twist.
- ⚠️ **The Trust was ruled an illegal monopoly** (US v. MPPC, 1 Oct 1915).
  Independents fled west to escape it, which is part of how Hollywood began. The
  film doesn't celebrate the cartel. It turns this into the payoff line:
  *"Edison needed a trust, a power plant and an army of linemen. You need
  Default."* Owning your own current is now open to every company, with no
  gatekeeper.
- ⚠️ **Legal check before air:** using Edison's name and likeness and the GE/Edison
  marks in a commercial needs a right-of-publicity and trademark review. A
  silhouette or an actor who is "never named on screen" is the safe fallback.
  The script's title cards say "1882" and "Pearl Street" and never print "Edison".

## 4. Default's product mix (what "the current" is made of)

From default.com and its launch coverage:

| Stage of the current | Default product | On-screen beat |
|---|---|---|
| Capture | **Forms** (branded, native), **Website intent / visitor ID** | A buyer's cursor hits "Book a demo" at 11:58 pm |
| Enrich | **Waterfall enrichment** across data sources, at the moment of form fill | Firmographics cascade down the screen like a lit fuse |
| Qualify | **AI qualification / scoring / segmentation** in the workflow | ICP fit lights up: green |
| Route | **Routing** for leads, meetings and accounts (round robin, territories, ownership) | A switchboard cable becomes a routing line |
| Schedule | **Scheduling** for sales, marketing and CS (one scheduling layer) | The calendar books before the tab closes |
| Orchestrate | **Workflows** canvas, **GTM data layer**, **Dot** (the revenue agent) | The orbital wire sculpture becomes the workflow graph |
| Own it | **Marketing CRM**, a single control layer that replaces point tools (e.g. Chili Piper, LeanData) | The city grid lights up block by block |

Proof points for the campaign: an 8VC-led Series A ($20M total, with Craft and
Jack Altman), and the Cortex case study, *"a single control layer for all GTM
tools."*
